About

I am an M.S. student in Data Science at the University of Chicago. Since August 2026 I have been a visiting student in the Department of Astrophysical Sciences at Princeton University, working with Dr. Chang-Goo Kim. I received my B.S. in Physics from Zhejiang University in 2025, where I wrote my thesis with Prof. Renyue Cen.

I use numerical simulations to study how structure forms and evolves, from the turbulent gas in galaxies to the cosmic web. I am applying to Ph.D. programs in astrophysics for Fall 2027.

Research interests

I am interested in the physics of structure formation across scales. I mainly work by building and analyzing simulations, and I want to test them against observations. I am also interested in connecting scales, for example using small-scale physics to set better priors for large-scale models.

  • Turbulence and stellar feedback in the multiphase interstellar medium
  • Feedback and self-regulation in galaxy formation
  • Gas in and around galaxies, from the ISM to the circumgalactic medium
  • Small-scale structure in ΛCDM and the Local Universe

Research

Turbulence in the multiphase ISM

2026–present

Princeton University, with Dr. Chang-Goo Kim and Dr. Rajsekhar Mohapatra

I decompose the turbulence in the TIGRESS simulations of the star-forming ISM into compressive and solenoidal modes with independent methods. I mask the hot gas before the decomposition to isolate the turbulent modes of the warm and cold phases, and relate them to feedback and star formation.

Thin disks as a function of baryonic mass

2025–present

The University of Chicago, with Prof. Andrey Kravtsov

I built a pipeline that cleans, deblends, and masks H i-selected dwarf galaxies in deep imaging. I measure their shapes and model how disk thickness depends on stellar mass, baryonic mass, and rotation velocity.

Planar structures in ΛCDM universes

2023–2026

With Prof. Renyue Cen, in collaboration with Prof. P. J. E. Peebles

I developed a geometric probe for planar structures and used Local Group analogs in cosmological N-body simulations to test how rare the observed local planar structures are in ΛCDM.

The quasar dipole in cosmological simulations

2026

Course project, Statistical Methods in Astrophysics

I built mock quasar skies for Local Group-like observers in large N-body simulations and tested how unusual the observed quasar dipole and its misalignment with the CMB dipole are in ΛCDM.
